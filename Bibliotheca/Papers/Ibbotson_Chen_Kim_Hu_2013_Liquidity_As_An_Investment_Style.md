# Ibbotson, Chen, Kim & Hu (2013) — Liquidity as an Investment Style

*The abstract only, with the citation checked against the publisher's record — not the full paper.*

## Why it is here

Claim 3 of [`OBJECTIVE.md`](../../OBJECTIVE.md) — *the top-quintile liquidity screen makes every
position exitable in a day at ordinary volume*.

The second source that argues against this strategy, and the reason claim 3 is worded as a cost
rather than a benefit.

## Liquidity is an investment style in its own right, alongside size, value and momentum

Sorting US equities on turnover produces a return spread that persists and that the paper argues
stands apart from the established styles — it is not size in disguise, nor value, nor momentum.

> **For claim 3:** our screen is a bet on a style, whether or not we meant it to be. Selecting the
> top liquidity quintile is a deliberate, permanent tilt, and calling it "just a tradability filter"
> would hide an exposure rather than declare one. It is declared here and in `OBJECTIVE.md` instead.

## Less liquid stocks have historically earned higher returns than more liquid ones

The direction is the one that matters for us: within the market, the *low*-turnover end has been the
better-performing end.

> **For claim 3:** the screen selects the low-return end of the style on purpose. This is why claim 3
> reads *"true by construction, untested as a source of return"* and why *what is not claimed* says
> plainly that if the book outperforms, the screen is not where the outperformance came from.
>
> **What the screen buys is capacity, exitability and net-of-cost survival** — the things that decide
> whether a result can be traded rather than whether it exists. Together with
> [Lesmond, Schill & Zhou (2004)](Lesmond_Schill_Zhou_2004_Illusory_Nature_Of_Momentum_Profits.md),
> the honest expectation for this strategy is: **lower gross return than an unscreened momentum
> book, and a better chance of keeping what is left after costs.** Those are two predictions, and
> `FINDINGS_1.md` should evaluate them separately.

## What it changes

- **The screen is declared as a style tilt**, deliberate and permanent, not "just a tradability
  filter".
- **Claim 3 is worded as a cost:** the screen selects the low-return end of the style on purpose,
  and if the book outperforms, the screen is not where that came from.
- **Two predictions for `FINDINGS_1.md`, evaluated separately:** lower gross return than an
  unscreened momentum book, and a better chance of keeping what is left after costs.
- **What the sort's shape looks like inside the liquid end is a sweep a later experiment owes.** The
  top quintile is a cut we chose for roundness, not one this paper endorses, so the cost of our
  specific threshold is not known from here.
- **Turnover and `c_dollar_volume_63d` are not interchangeable.** The paper sorts on turnover and
  ours is traded value; they are related and not identical, and a note may not quietly substitute
  one for the other. If the distinction ever matters to a conclusion, it has to be measured, not
  assumed.
- **Does not settle** anything beyond one market and one era — the same caveat as every other note
  in this folder.
